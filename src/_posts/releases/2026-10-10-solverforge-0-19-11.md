## What Changed

### Complete buffered Clarke-Wright merges survive interruption

When construction is interrupted after successful merges, the solver can publish
that complete buffered route assignment instead of discarding the refinement and
leaving the singleton seed. Assignment reuses the merge loop's owner-feasibility
evidence and metric-class matching without extending the search budget or probing
owner feasibility again. If that evidence cannot place every buffered route, the
committed complete assignment is retained. Interruption during savings generation
also preserves the committed complete assignment.

Regression tests cover configured termination and cancellation after merges,
complete element coverage, and applied move telemetry for retained refinement.
This is not permission to publish an incomplete solution or ignore control limits.

### Relational buffers are reused across incremental updates

Retired flatten child-handle vectors, existence link lists, and hash buckets are
kept for reuse after retraction. Existence probes reuse scratch buffers. Internal
maps use deterministic hashing and generation-checked handle side tables; concrete
operator composition and exact old-state retraction remain unchanged. Reused
storage does not make a stale row generation valid.

### Exact indexed equality avoids redundant extraction

Candidates from typed equality buckets are already equal, so `ViewEqualPlan`
does not invoke both key extractors again for each candidate pair. Direct
`matches` still compares extracted keys. The regression test pins key-call counts
for full evaluation; this is an exact indexed equality path, not a blanket bypass
of residual predicates on other relationship plans.

These implementation changes are not new benchmark measurements. No throughput,
latency, or allocation reduction is claimed for the published site workload;
historical benchmark evidence and tested versions remain unchanged.

  applied telemetry, not just the terminal reason.

  semi/anti join residual predicates change.
